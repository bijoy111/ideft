The Core Result: 16 Hosts

10 primaries and 6 hosts that can act as backups, 2 of them deliberately degraded. This is the scenario the paper is actually about: does knowing which hosts are bad change anything once hosts differ in quality?

Re-run on bare metal. This scenario has since been repeated on ten networked machines, each backup pinned to its own physical core, with degraded hosts slowed by the Linux kernel rather than by a sleep — see Bare Metal. The ordering below holds there. The absolute latencies do not: the p99 figures on this page sit on a one-second plateau caused by DEFT's five-deep accept queue, not by backup placement.
XML-RPC: 5 seeds × 3 trials (15 runs) gRPC: 5 seeds × 5 trials (25 runs) 200 calls/primary, burst=10 2 of 6 backups degraded
Two transports, and why that matters. Every method is run twice: once over DEFT's shipped XML-RPC path, and once over gRPC with persistent HTTP/2 channels, which is how production NFV systems carry state replication. Everything else is held fixed, so the transport is a controlled variable. DEFT's backup is an HTTP/1.0 SimpleXMLRPCServer with a five-deep accept backlog, so every call opens a fresh TCP connection; when that backlog overflows the latency tail lands on the kernel's SYN-retransmission timer rather than on queueing delay. That makes p99 unusable on the XML-RPC arm — it reads either tens of milliseconds or about 1050 ms depending only on whether a run's SYN-drop rate crosses 1% — so we report mean and p95. The XML-RPC transport also inflates the measured placement effect by roughly 4.3×. See the 16-host page for the distribution comparison.

Statistical Comparison

Statistics are run-level: one independent observation per (method, context seed, trial), compared with a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test and Holm-Bonferroni correction across the family, with cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals that resample runs rather than individual calls. Pooling individual RPC calls, as an earlier version of this site did, treats thousands of correlated requests against one server process as independent samples and drives p-values to zero without adding evidence.

The Two Transports Side by Side

Complementary CDF of per-call service latency, both transports, log–log. The XML-RPC tail is a staircase of kernel timer quanta rather than a queueing curve: 3.20% of calls land beyond 900 ms, against 0.013% under gRPC — a 240× difference in tail mass. The maxima are 22,773 ms and 1,139 ms.

What this means: once hosts differ in quality, feature-aware selection wins, and it wins on both transports. heuristic, optimal and PPO never route a primary to a degraded host in any seed; round_robin hits one on 2 of 10 assignments in every seed, because it cycles blindly with no way to know which hosts are bad; random is inconsistent, and its confidence interval is far wider than anyone else's — random placement is not merely mediocre on average, it is unstable. The three feature-aware methods are statistically indistinguishable from one another, so the cheap deterministic heuristic matches the exact optimum.
