\section{Online Backup Selection under Churn}
\label{sec:online}

Every method so far solves a \emph{one-shot} problem: given a snapshot of host
state, choose an assignment. Host state is not static. Hosts degrade, recover
and fail, and moving a primary's backup is not free, because the new backup
holds no replica and a migration costs a full state transfer. This section asks
what adaptation is worth under those conditions. It is a smaller contribution
than \S\ref{sec:results}, and we state it compactly: proofs are in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Formulation}
\label{sec:online:model}

Let $P$ be the primaries and $k$ the number of feasible backups for a primary.
At round $t$, assigning $p$ to $b$ incurs a service cost $c_t(p,b) \ge 0$, which
we instantiate with the weighted feature score of \S\ref{sec:heuristic}, negated
and shifted to be non-negative, so the online objective is the online version of
the paper's objective rather than a new one. For an assignment $\sigma_t$ with
load $\ell_t(b)$ on host $b$, the cost of round $t$ is

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{cost}_t(\sigma_t) = \sum_{p \in P} \Big[\;
  & c_t(p, \sigma_t(p))
  + \lambda\big(\ell_t(\sigma_t(p)) - 1\big) \\
  & + M \cdot \mathbb{1}[\sigma_t(p) \neq \sigma_{t-1}(p)] \Big],
\end{split}
\label{eq:roundcost}
\end{equation}

where $\lambda$ charges congestion when primaries share a backup and $M$ is the
migration cost in units of one round of service. A migration decided at round
$t$ takes effect at $t+1$: a primary cannot retroactively hold state on the new
backup. An algorithm is $\rho$-competitive if its cost is within $\rho$ of the
offline optimum on every instance.

Two quantities make the results readable. The \emph{floor}
$F = \sum_t \sum_p \min_b c_t(p,b)$ is what no policy, including the optimum,
can avoid paying. The \emph{excess ratio}
$(\mathrm{cost}(A) - F)/(\mathrm{cost}(\mathrm{OPT}) - F)$ isolates the part of
the bill selection actually controls, and is what we report.

\subsection{Three Results}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:snapshot}
Let \textsc{snapshot} be the policy that plays a cost-minimizing assignment for
round $t$ at every $t$. For every $M > 0$ and every $\rho$, there is an instance on
which $\mathrm{cost}(\textsc{snapshot}) > \rho \cdot \mathrm{cost}(\mathrm{OPT})$.
\textsc{snapshot} is therefore not $\rho$-competitive for any $\rho$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
The construction needs no churn in the usual sense: it is driven by fluctuations
too small to be worth acting on. The failure is not that \textsc{snapshot}
chooses badly --- it chooses the best host every round --- but that it cannot
decline a move whose benefit does not repay its cost. This matters here because
re-running the exact assignment of \S\ref{sec:optimal} as conditions change is
the natural way to deploy it.

\textsc{hysteresis}($\alpha$) accumulates each primary's regret since its last
migration and moves only once that regret reaches $\alpha M$, at which point the
counter resets. The threshold is in units of $M$, so the policy needs no
retuning when state size or link bandwidth changes.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:budget}
On every instance, \textsc{hysteresis}($\alpha$) spends at most a $1/\alpha$
fraction of its own service-plus-congestion cost on migration.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
This is a bound on \emph{waste} rather than on competitiveness: the policy
cannot be made to thrash, whatever the adversary does, and the bound holds on
every instance rather than in expectation.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:lower}
With $|P| = 1$ and $\lambda = 0$, online backup selection is exactly the metrical
task system (MTS) problem on the uniform metric over $k$ states with switching cost
$M$. Consequently no deterministic online algorithm is better than
$(2k-1)$-competitive, and no randomized algorithm is better than
$\Omega(\log k / \log\log k)$-competitive against an oblivious adversary.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
Two consequences matter in practice. A constant-factor guarantee is not a
reasonable design target at NFV scales, so a budget guarantee is the right kind
of promise. And the known $(2k-1)$-competitive deterministic and
$O(\log^2 k)$ randomized algorithms for uniform MTS are available for this
problem, though both maintain state proportional to the candidate set; we do not
deploy them, because the per-primary counter is already close to the offline
optimum on realistic traces.
