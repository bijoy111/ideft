128 Hosts: Where a Single Machine Runs Out

80 primaries and 48 backup-eligible hosts, 16 of them degraded. At this size one machine can no longer produce a trustworthy latency measurement, on either transport, so we report no latency numbers here — only results computed from the produced mapping, which do not depend on timing at all.

5 seeds × 3 trials (15 runs) 200 calls/primary, burst=10 16 of 48 backups degraded latency withdrawn
Why the latency numbers are withdrawn. 80 primaries at burst 10 is 800 client threads, and on the gRPC arm roughly 75 backup processes with eight workers each add some 600 server threads — on 8 cores. Two independent executions of the identical gRPC configuration differ by 4.8× on optimal and 6.1× on PPO, with confidence intervals spanning an order of magnitude. That is the machine being measured, not the placement policy. An earlier version of this page published those numbers; it should not have.
This has since been measured properly. The scenario was repeated on ten bare-metal nodes, 128 backup servers with one physical core each, degraded hosts slowed by the Linux kernel rather than by a sleep — see Bare Metal. The structural reading below is confirmed there, but two magnitudes change sharply: with identical hosts no selection method beats random, and most of the apparent cost of concentrating backups turns out to be DEFT's five-deep accept queue rather than load on the backup. Read this page as the single-machine account and that one as the measurement.
What does survive. Two things. First, gross concentration: concentrated is worse than every other method with r = 1.000 on both transports and in both executions, and on the XML-RPC arm it is the only configuration where client retries contribute measurably at all — 3.53% of calls retry, and 19.6 s of its 35.1 s wall-clock p99 is the client's own backoff sleep rather than backup delay. Second, the structure of the mapping, below.

Computed from the assignment, so this is exact and hardware-independent. It is the quantity the 128-host scenario was built to test: whether a policy spreads 80 primaries across the available pool or funnels them onto a few hosts.

What this means: the deterministic heuristic spreads 80 primaries across nearly as many distinct backups, while the learned policy does not. PPO's original checkpoint (library-default entropy, a [64,64] network) collapsed to 1–5 distinct backups out of 80; retraining with entropy 0.02, a [256,256] network and 1M timesteps widened the spread substantially, and both checkpoints are published. The residual gap traces to the reward: host quality is labelled explicitly by the health and capacity terms, whereas concentration is discouraged by a direct but modest penalty whose effect saturates quickly — see the ablation in the paper.
