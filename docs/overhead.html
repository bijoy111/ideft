Selection Overhead

If the system is going to add and remove hosts while running, the method that picks backups has to be fast. A method that picks perfectly but takes seconds to decide is much less useful than one that is nearly as good and answers straight away. This page asks a different question from the others: not "how good is the mapping" but "how long does it take to work it out."

Conclusion: every method is far quicker than the time a system normally takes to even notice a failure, which is usually hundreds of milliseconds to a few seconds. rl is always the slowest, because it runs the neural network once for each primary on top of the usual library overhead. Its jump at 128 hosts comes mainly from the bigger network needed to fix the collapse described on the 128-host page, not just from having more primaries to go through. Even so, the time to pick backups is not what holds things back at 128 hosts. As that page shows, the real limit is how evenly rl spreads load, not how fast it decides.
