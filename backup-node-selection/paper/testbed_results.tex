\subsection{Bare-Metal Validation at 128 Hosts}
\label{sec:testbed}

\S\ref{sec:rl128} withheld every latency claim at 128 hosts because a single
machine cannot produce one: 80 primaries at burst 10 contend with the backups
they are measuring. This section supplies the measurement on hardware where
that contention does not exist, and in doing so finds two further reasons the
single-machine numbers could not have been trusted.

\textbf{Testbed.} Eleven bare-metal CloudLab nodes at the Utah cluster
(\texttt{c6525-25g}: one 16-core AMD EPYC 7302P, 128\,GB, 25\,GbE, Ubuntu
22.04). Ten carry the 128 backup servers, thirteen per node except the last;
the eleventh runs the client alone. Each server is launched under
\texttt{taskset} on a core of its own. Hyperthread siblings on this part pair
as $(0,16), (1,17), \dots$, so logical CPUs $2$--$14$ are fourteen distinct
physical cores and no two servers share one core's two threads. The claim that
each backup owned a core is checked rather than asserted: the provisioning tool
records the intended core in a manifest and a verification pass reports the
affinity each server actually holds, together with the ports it answers on. All
replication traffic crosses the 25\,GbE experiment LAN; the control network is
not used for measurement.

\textbf{The accept-backlog pathology, tested directly.} \S\ref{sec:p99}
identifies DEFT's five-deep accept backlog as the cause of the latency tail and
infers the mechanism from the shape of the distribution and from the contrast
with gRPC. The testbed permits the direct test that a single machine did not:
hold every other variable fixed, change only that integer, and read the kernel's
own counters.

Reproducing DEFT as shipped gives a result that cannot be about placement. Four
methods whose p95 differs by less than 2\,ms have p99 values within 12\,ms of
$1040$\,ms; a constant surviving every change of
method is an artifact, and one second is the Linux initial retransmission
timeout. The counters confirm the inferred mechanism rather than merely being
consistent with it: $103{,}209$ \texttt{ListenOverflows} on the most heavily
selected node against $116$ retransmitted segments, with \texttt{ListenDrops}
equal to \texttt{ListenOverflows} exactly. The connections were refused at the
server, not lost in the network.

Raising the backlog to $512$ drove the overflow count to zero and p99 from
$1040$\,ms to $79$\,ms. The consequence for the placement result is larger than
the tail alone suggests (Table~\ref{tab:loadcurve}): under the shipped backlog,
mean latency rises $7.5\times$ from one primary per backup to seventy-nine,
which invites the reading that concentration is catastrophic; with a backlog of
512 the same sweep costs $35\%$. Most of the apparent penalty for sharing a
backup was connection setup being refused rather than queueing at the backup.
This sharpens \S\ref{sec:p99}'s estimate that the shipped transport inflates the
measured concentration penalty: the inflation is attributable to one
configurable integer, and removing it leaves an effect that is real but
considerably smaller.

\textbf{The bias does not have a single sign.} Repeating the 16-host scenario on
the same nodes with the same \texttt{tc} degradation, changing only the backlog,
gives the opposite result (Table~\ref{tab:backlog16}). The one-second plateau
appears exactly as before --- p99 of $1013$--$1023$\,ms across four methods whose
p95 spans $28$ to $102$\,ms --- but the \emph{gap between methods} is
\emph{larger} once the backlog is fixed: \texttt{round\_robin} minus
\texttt{heuristic} is $3.18$\,ms as shipped and $6.07$\,ms at backlog 512. At
this scale the shipped queue understates the placement effect rather than
inflating it.

The two results are consistent once the mechanism is stated. Overflow is driven
by how many primaries share one backup. At 128 hosts with 80 primaries,
concentration is extreme and the overflow penalty falls hardest on exactly the
placements that are already worst, exaggerating the spread. At 16 hosts with 10
primaries over 16 backups nothing is concentrated enough for that, so the
penalty falls roughly evenly and compresses the spread instead. The shipped
backlog therefore \emph{biases} a measured placement effect, with a sign that
depends on how concentrated the workload is --- which is a stronger reason to
report it as a controlled variable than if it merely scaled everything.

A third observation falls out of the same table and is a property of the shipped
system rather than of our measurement: the five-deep queue \emph{lowers} the
median, from $22.68$ to $14.64$\,ms for \texttt{optimal}. A short accept queue is
admission control. Calls that are accepted contend with fewer others for a
single-threaded server and complete faster; calls that are refused wait a full
second. DEFT as shipped trades a better median for a catastrophic tail.

\textbf{With homogeneous hosts, selection does not matter.}
On identical hardware with no degraded hosts, \texttt{optimal},
\texttt{heuristic}, \texttt{round\_robin} and \texttt{random} are statistically
indistinguishable from one another at $41.5$--$42.0$\,ms mean
with no method separating from the others. The paired comparison between
\texttt{optimal} and \texttt{random} gives a median difference of $0.002$\,ms
($p_{\text{Holm}}=1.000$). Only \texttt{concentrated} ($56.27$\,ms) and
\texttt{PPO} ($46.53$\,ms) separate from the group. This is the expected result
and worth stating plainly: where every candidate is equivalent, a selector that
reads node features has nothing to read, and the Hungarian assignment buys
nothing over choosing uniformly at random.

\textbf{Degradation is imposed by the kernel, not by the server.}
The deployment refuses to simulate a degraded backup by sleeping inside a
server it did not start, so the heterogeneous arm slows hosts physically.
\texttt{tc netem} attaches to an interface, but a node here carries thirteen
backups and shaping the interface would degrade their healthy co-tenants, which
the comparison depends on as controls. Each backup owns a distinct TCP port, so
a \texttt{prio} qdisc with a \texttt{u32} filter on source port delays exactly
the degraded servers. The fourth band of a four-band \texttt{prio} is
unreachable through the default priomap, so healthy flows never enter the netem
queue at all rather than being delayed by zero. Measured on adjacent ports of
one interface, a degraded backup costs $101.4$\,ms per call and its healthy
neighbour $1.0$\,ms. The $101.4$ is itself informative: \texttt{netem} delays
every packet the server sends, so a per-connection protocol pays the added
latency twice, once on the SYN-ACK and once on the response.

\textbf{With degraded hosts, feature-aware selection earns its place.}
Sixteen of the 128 hosts degraded, \texttt{heuristic} and \texttt{optimal}
route a primary to a degraded backup in $0.00\%$ of calls, while
\texttt{round\_robin} reaches them at the base rate of $12.50\%$ and
\texttt{random} at $11.25\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:scales}). The latency
follows the selection: $42.0$ and $41.8$\,ms against $51.9$ and $51.3$, and at
p99 $81$ and $80$\,ms against $157$ and $155$. Every one of these comparisons
has $r=-1.000$ over 15 paired runs, meaning no run dissents. The gap between
\texttt{heuristic} and \texttt{optimal} is not significant on either mean
($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.909$) or p99 ($p_{\text{Holm}}=1.000$), so the
multi-criteria heuristic attains the quality of the capacity-constrained
Hungarian assignment without solving one.

\textbf{The result holds at every scale we can run on this hardware.}
Repeating the degraded scenario at 16 and 32 hosts, with 12.5\% of hosts
degraded at each, gives the same ordering and the same mechanism
(Table~\ref{tab:scales}). \texttt{heuristic} and \texttt{optimal} reach a
degraded backup in $0.00\%$ of calls at all three scales; \texttt{round\_robin}
meets them at $10.00$--$12.50\%$ and \texttt{random} at $11.25$--$18.00\%$. The
latency gap between the two groups widens with the pool, from $6.72$\,ms at 16
hosts to $7.38$ at 32 and $10.11$ at 128, which is what one expects as blind
selection has more degraded hosts to encounter. \texttt{heuristic} versus
\texttt{optimal} is not significant at any scale.

These are the scales the paper previously measured on a single machine
(\S\ref{sec:scale32}), so the comparison also settles what that arrangement
cost. At 16 hosts the single-machine run reported a p99 of $1110$--$1136$\,ms
for the four leading methods; the same scenario here reports $49$--$121$\,ms.
The one-second plateau was never specific to 128 hosts --- it has bounded p99 at
every scale in this paper, which is visible only once the backlog can be varied.

\textbf{A contiguous degraded block makes the baselines look better than they
are.} An earlier execution of this arm degraded the first sixteen
backup-eligible hosts, \texttt{w81}--\texttt{w96}, and reported
\texttt{round\_robin} tied with \texttt{optimal} at $41.3$\,ms having selected a
degraded host $0\%$ of the time. That tie was an artifact of naming.
\texttt{round\_robin} cycles a lexicographically sorted host list, in which
every name beginning \texttt{w1}--\texttt{w7} precedes \texttt{w8}; with 80
primaries the cursor advances 80 positions and never reaches the block. The
method scored zero by never arriving, not by avoiding. Spreading the same
sixteen degraded hosts evenly along that ordering puts them inside every
method's reach, after which \texttt{round\_robin} meets them at the base rate
and its mean rises from $41.28$ to $51.87$\,ms. We report the spread
configuration and note the contiguous one only as a caution: a load-spreading
baseline evaluated against a contiguous impairment can appear to avoid it.

\textbf{PPO avoids degraded hosts but does not spread load.} The learned policy
selects a degraded backup $0.00\%$ of the time, matching the deterministic
methods, and is nonetheless significantly slower than both
($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0009$ against \texttt{heuristic}, $r=1.000$). The structural
measurement explains it: across this run PPO used 44 distinct backups where
\texttt{heuristic} used 112 and \texttt{random} 119. Host-quality
discrimination and load balancing are separable, and PPO acquired the first
without the second, which is the same split \S\ref{sec:rl128} reports
structurally and Table~\ref{tab:rlablation} traces to the anti-concentration
term.

\textbf{What the concentrated arm now measures.} Under the spread
configuration the host \texttt{concentrated} piles onto is itself degraded, so
its $260$\,ms conflates concentration with degradation and is not a clean
measure of either. The clean figure for concentration alone is the homogeneous
column: $56.27$\,ms against $41.73$, a penalty of $35\%$ rather than the
$7.4\times$ the as-shipped run reported.
